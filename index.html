A field guide for people who learn by doing

Stop re-learning every deal.
Give it a second brain.

A second brain is a folder of plain notes on your own computer that links together and grows every time you use it. Pair it with an AI assistant and it turns into a business partner who remembers every tract, every county office, every timber buyer, and every lesson you paid for.

What it is

Notes that remember for you

The tool is Obsidian, a free notes app. Every note is an ordinary text file in a folder you own. No subscription or cloud lock-in is required, and it still opens in twenty years.

What makes it a brain instead of a filing cabinet is the links. When you type [[Ware County]] inside a note about a tract, the two notes are connected. Later the Ware County note shows every tract, contact and permit that ever touched it, without any re-filing.

The AI piece is newer. Assistants like Claude can now read and write the folder directly. You drop in raw material such as a voice memo, a cruise report, a closing statement or a photo of a plat. The AI files it, links it, and answers questions from it later, citing the note it got the answer from.

Why anyone should have one

Benefits that compound

Most people's knowledge sits in their head, their texts and a truck full of folders. A second brain collects it in one place that you can search, and it gets more valuable with every note.

01

Capture once, use forever

You write the lesson down the day you learn it. Next year's deal starts from what you know rather than from a blank page.

02

An AI that knows your business

A generic chatbot knows timber in general. Yours also knows your buyers, your margins, your counties and the ways your past deals went sideways.

03

Answers you can check

Each answer points back to the note and source it came from. Because you can check it, you can trust it.

04

You own it

The notes are plain files on your machine. You can switch AI tools any time and the knowledge comes with you.

05

Easy to hand off

A partner, a new hire or your family can open the folder and see how the business runs. Nothing is lost if one person is out.

06

Patterns show up

Once ten tracts sit side by side you start to see which counties move fast, which buyers pay best and how big a lot sells quickest.

For a timber-and-land business

Every stage of the deal gets a memory

Buying timbered tracts in southeast Georgia, harvesting, then cutting the land into plots is a long, multi-year process with a lot of different people involved. Details are easy to lose along the way. Here's what the second brain holds at each stage.

  1. Find

    Leads, sellers, asking price, why it's for sale, first impressions from the drive-by.

  2. Diligence

    Title, survey, access, wetlands, flood zone, soils, any conservation-use covenant, the timber cruise.

  3. Close

    Final numbers, the attorney, the lender, the documents, and what surprised you.

  4. Harvest

    Logging contractor, timber buyer, mill tickets, county notices, best-practice notes and how it really went.

  5. Divide

    Surveyor, county planning and plat approval, road frontage, perc tests, lot lines and pricing.

  6. Sell

    Listings, buyer inquiries, offers, terms, what sold fast and what sat on the market.

Getting started

Start in an afternoon

You don't need to be technical. Start small, with one tract you know well.

  1. Install Obsidian

    Free from obsidian.md. Create a new vault, which is just a folder, and call it something like Land Brain.

  2. Pick an AI that can work with files

    The best fit is an assistant that can read and write the folder itself, like Claude Code or the Claude desktop app with folder access. A regular chat AI also works if you paste notes in and copy its answers back.

  3. Give it the starter file

    Download the starter file below, put it in the vault, and tell the AI: "Read land-second-brain-starter.md and set me up." It will interview you, build the folders and templates, and write your first notes.

  4. Feed it one real deal

    Drop in everything you have on a single tract: notes, PDFs, photos, a voice memo. Ask the AI to file it. Then ask it a question and check the answer.

  5. Make it a habit

    After any call, site visit or closing, spend two minutes dropping a note in the inbox. Once a week, ask the AI to file the inbox and tell you what it learned.

Good habits from the start

  • The AI organizes; you decide. Treat its notes as drafts until you've checked them, especially numbers.
  • It is not your attorney, forester or CPA. It helps you remember what they told you and what to ask them next time. Covenants, taxes, permits and plat rules change and differ by county, so confirm with the professional.
  • Keep sensitive files private. Bank details, IDs and anything confidential from a seller don't belong in the notes, and you don't need them there.
  • Back it up. It's a folder. Copy it to an external drive, or use Obsidian Sync or iCloud.
